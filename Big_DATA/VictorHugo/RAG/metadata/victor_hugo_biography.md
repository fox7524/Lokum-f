# Victor Hugo biography summary

Victor-Marie Hugo (1802-1885) was a French poet, novelist, dramatist, essayist, journalist, activist, and politician.
He emerged from the Romantic generation, became a leading public writer in France, moved from youthful royalism toward republican commitments, and spent years in exile after opposing Louis-Napoléon's coup.

## Major identity anchors
- born in Besançon on 26 February 1802
- died in Paris on 22 May 1885
- associated with French Romanticism
- best known internationally for `Les Misérables` and `Notre-Dame de Paris`
- central public causes: opposition to tyranny, abolition of the death penalty, defense of the poor, anti-slavery statements, education, and republican civic life

## Personal anchors
- parents: Joseph Léopold Sigisbert Hugo and Sophie Trébuchet
- spouse: Adèle Foucher
- children: Léopoldine, Charles, François-Victor, Adèle, and Léopold
- intimate long-term companion: Juliette Drouet

## Artistic range
Hugo wrote poetry, novels, plays, political prose, speeches, travel writing, notebooks, and letters.
He also produced thousands of drawings and often treated architecture, landscape, and public space as historical and moral symbols.

## Political shape
Hugo's career moves through monarchy, revolution, assembly politics, exile, and the republican order.
He used literature and oratory as public intervention, not just artistic production.
