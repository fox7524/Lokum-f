# Address to the Workman's Congress at Marseille (1879)

Source page: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Victor_Hugo%27s_Address_to_the_Workman%27s_Congress_at_Marseille

This speech is a compact map of Hugo's late political idealism. It links labor, republicanism, international fraternity, technological progress, anti-war feeling, and a near-prophetic image of the coming century.

Notable ideas:
- the social question is the question of labor
- the Republic is presented as settled political ground
- the future should abolish war, the scaffold, and domination
- work and thought are paired as human destiny
